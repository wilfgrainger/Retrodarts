Getting started

What is Retro Darts Scorer?

A free darts scoreboard and scorer for pub teams and leagues. It shows big LED scores for 301, 401 and 501, does the arithmetic for every visit and gives you a PDF match report. It works offline and needs no account. Matches are saved on your device, and if you sign in with Google they are also backed up to your own Google Drive.

Is it free?

Yes. It is free to use, with no adverts. There is nothing to install or sign up for, and it works offline; signing in with Google is optional and adds backup.

How do I start a match?

Open retrodarts.com and tap Start a match. No account is needed: signing in with Google, from Settings, only backs matches up to your own Drive. Type the home and away names, choose 301, 401 or 501 and the fixture format, tap Start match and tell it who won the bull-up. After each visit key in the total scored and press Submit.

Which games can it score?

It scores 301, 401 and 501 for two sides, home and away, which can be teams or two players. You choose the fixture format (play all games, best of, or first to) and the number of legs per game (best of or first to).

Can I score singles or doubles?

Yes. It keeps two sides and records one visit total at a time, so name the sides as players or pairs and enter each visit in turn.

Scoring and league rules

Does it follow league scoring rules?

It follows generic pub darts scoring and deliberately keeps it simple: visits of three darts, a maximum of 180 per visit, a bust for going below zero or leaving 1, and the leg won on reaching exactly zero. Leagues differ on fixture format and house rules, so set the format to match yours and let your captains and league rules settle the rest.

Our league needs 5 games to win a fixture. Can it do that?

Yes. Games can be set to play all, best of, or first to. A best of 9 or first to 5 fixture ends as soon as one side reaches 5 games. A night of 8 games played in full ends 4-4 as a draw if the sides finish level. Legs per game can be best of or first to, for example best of 3.

Example: how does it line up with the Shrewsbury and District Darts League rules?

Rules differ between leagues, so always check yours, but this shows how it works in practice. That league's published rules include games of 301, best of three legs; a bust when the number required is exceeded or only one is required; a player may be told the score or the number required but not how to get it, and the double required may not be shown; electronic scoreboards allowed provided the last five scores are displayed; and 8-0 forfeit scores, which fit an eight-game night. Retro Darts Scorer lines up with that: it supports 301 and 401, best of three legs and eight games played in full, busts on exceeding the score or leaving 1, and shows what a side has left plus the last five visits. Checkout hints can be turned off in Settings, Game settings, so the double required is not shown. It does not check double start or double finish and has no forfeit entry, so forfeits go on the result card; who throws first is yours to decide, and the checker and marker stay the match officials. This is an example from the league's published rules, not an endorsement by the league.

Does it show checkouts?

Yes, unless you turn them off. With checkout hints on, the score box suggests a standard double-out finish, such as T20 T20 Bull for 170, whenever the throwing side has 170 or less. If your league does not allow the finish to be shown, turn them off in Settings, Game settings. It is only a suggestion: the app still accepts any total and does not check doubles.

How does a bust work?

If a visit scores more than the player has left, or would leave them on 1, it is a bust: the visit does not count, the score stays where it was and the turn passes. Reaching exactly zero wins the leg. A single visit can be at most 180.

Does it check that I finished on a double?

No. The scorer takes the total for each visit, not each dart, so it cannot see the last dart. It treats a visit that leaves exactly 1 as a bust because 1 cannot be finished on a double, and it ends the leg when a visit takes the score to exactly zero. Checking double in, double out and the finishing dart is left to the players, the captains and your league's rules.

Who throws first?

You tell the scorer. It asks who won the bull-up at the start of each game, whichever way your league decides who starts, such as a bull-up or a dart each at the board. Legs alternate who throws first, and a deciding leg asks again.

How is the average calculated?

The average is the three-dart average: points scored multiplied by three, divided by the darts thrown, with every visit counted as three darts. Because the scorer does not record how many darts a finishing visit used, a leg finished with fewer than three darts is still counted as three, so averages can read slightly lower than a count of actual darts.

Does it work out league points or tables?

No. It scores the fixture and produces a PDF match report of the result. League points, tables and fixtures are kept by your league.

On match night

How do I see who is throwing?

The side that is up has a full-width THROWING NOW bar and a bold frame, and the other side shows WAITING. Both big scores are always the same size so the whole room can read them.

Can I undo a wrong score?

Yes. Undo reverses the last visit after a confirmation, and Undo finish reopens a leg, game or fixture that was ended by mistake.

What if the tablet sleeps or I reload the page?

The current match is saved on the device after every visit and comes back after a reload or a sleeping screen.

Reports, data and devices

Can I get a PDF match report?

Yes. At the end of a leg, game or fixture you can download a PDF with the fixture result, every game and leg, each visit, highest scores and highest finishes. It is created on your device.

Where is my match data stored?

On the device you score on, in your browser: the current match is saved after every visit, and finished matches go into Match history. The site has no database, so nothing is saved on our side and we never see your games. If you sign in with Google, finished matches and your theme are also copied to a private app folder in your own Google Drive so they appear on your other devices; a match in progress stays on its device until it finishes. If you clear the browser's data or move to a new device, signing in again restores your finished matches from Drive.

Can I back up my matches to Google Drive?

Yes, it happens automatically. Sign in with Google on each device you use, and your finished matches, theme and settings then back up to a private app folder in your own Google Drive and appear on your other devices. Only Retro Darts Scorer can use that folder; you can see it, or delete it, in Google Drive under Settings, Manage apps. If a device has no signal the first time, you can still score and sign in next time it is online.

Does the site use cookies?

Only if you allow it. A banner asks whether to use Google Analytics, which counts visits and which features get used, such as matches started and PDFs downloaded. Team names and scores are never sent. Reject it and no analytics cookies are set. You can change your choice under Cookie settings in the menu.

Can I show the scoreboard on the pub TV?

Yes, with a Chromecast or a TV that has Chromecast built in. Open the scorer in Chrome on an Android phone or tablet, or on a computer, tap Cast to TV and pick the TV. The TV opens its own scoreboard and the scorer sends it only the scores, not video, so it keeps up on slow pub Wi-Fi. The scorer and the TV need to be on the same Wi-Fi.

Does the darts scorer work offline?

Yes. Open it once on each device with a connection; after that it is stored on the device and works with no signal: scoring, Match history and the PDF all work offline, and closing or reloading the app is fine. The FAQ pages need a connection. If you are signed in, matches scored offline back up automatically the next time you use the app with signal. If a brand-new device has no signal at all, you can still score.

What devices and themes are there?

Any modern browser on a phone, tablet or computer. It is designed so the whole room can read the score from the oche, for example on a tablet mounted on the wall. There are four themes, Retro, Retro Black, Modern and Classical Pub, which share one layout and differ only in colour. Choose one from the menu and this page follows it.
